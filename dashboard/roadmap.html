01 Roadmap

Sellable comes before finished.

One person, building full-time. The goal is not to complete the MVP checklist — it is to reach the earliest point where an operator will pay, then let their pilot fund the rest. These three gates are ordered by what each one lets you sell, not by what is easiest to build.

Estimates for one experienced full-stack developer working full-time, starting from the current prototype: a verified webhook and working outbound test, with no durable memory, handoff, or inbox. Nothing here has been measured. Part-time, multiply by roughly 2.5.

02 Gate 1

A demo that survives a skeptical operator

The purpose of these four weeks is not a product. It is a conversation you can run live in front of someone who rents Lamborghinis for a living, without anything embarrassing happening. This is where you stop spending your own money — not where recurring revenue starts.

WEEKS 1–4 Build

Durable transport, a minimal inbox, AI drafting on a test number

Everything here exists to make the demo real rather than staged. Messages survive a restart, nothing is sent twice, and the inbox shows what a salesperson would actually see.

BUILD
  • Webhook signature check, save before acknowledging, event deduplication
  • Outbox, queue, and a single dispatcher that owns all sending
  • Conversation and message storage that survives a restart
  • A thread view with manual reply and takeover
  • AI drafting replies from a small set of hand-written answers
SKIP ENTIRELY
  • Approved knowledge management and publishing
  • Follow-ups, reporting, quote calculation
  • Multi-operator isolation and role permissions
  • Anything in the Operations MVP
WHAT THIS LETS YOU SELL

Not a subscription — nothing has touched a real customer yet, and selling a monthly fee for software that does not exist creates an obligation you may not be able to meet. But there are three ways to take money in this window.

01 Run it as a service, by handOne operator forwards their enquiries; you answer fast using the drafts, from the inbox you just built. They pay for the outcome — replies in minutes, nothing lost overnight — not for software. You are the human in shadow mode, and you learn their real questions, objections and rates while being paid to do it.
02 A paid discovery engagementMap their enquiry volume, current response times, and what is being lost at night and on weekends. Valuable to them independent of your product, and it ends with a number that becomes your own sales asset. It also filters seriousness: an operator who will not pay for discovery will not pay for a pilot.
03 A setup fee against a committed pilotThe design partner signs, pays onboarding now, and the subscription starts when they go live at Gate 2. Keep this cheque small and the scope explicit — if Gate 2 slips, you owe someone something.

Verification may constrain this. If Meta business verification has not cleared by week 4 you cannot run on their number, which limits the service option to a number you control — or to you replying from their existing phone with the system behind you. Know that before promising a start date.

Do not anchor on the research pricing yet. The AED 1,000–2,000 Starter range is marked directional and unvalidated in the market research. Price a first cheque as what it actually is — a discovery or a service month — not as a discount off a subscription you have not proven.

03 Gate 2

One operator live, supervised

Their knowledge, their number, their salespeople — running in shadow mode, where the AI drafts and a person sends every message.

WEEKS 5–11 Build

The five qualifying fields, the handoff packet, and a request queue

This is the Sales MVP narrowed to what one pilot operator actually needs, plus the thinnest slice of Operations that lets a person answer an availability question inside the system instead of over the phone.

BUILD
  • Their approved FAQs, rates, requirements and delivery areas, loaded and versioned
  • Extraction of the five qualifying fields, with dates resolved against their timezone
  • The handoff packet, queue, owner and next action
  • The Operations request queue and a console to answer it
  • Voice notes and photos acknowledged and routed to a person
  • The eight safety rules, enforced in the backend rather than the prompt
STILL SKIP
  • Quote calculation and approval workflow
  • Follow-up automation
  • Booking creation, payments, documents
  • Arabic and mixed-language handling
  • Reporting dashboards
WHAT THIS LETS YOU SELL

The pilot itself, at roughly the Starter price the market research proposes for a small operator.

Shadow mode is not a limitation to apologise for. “Your team approves every message — the AI just makes sure nothing waits” is an easier sell to someone whose brand is the product than full autonomy would be, and it is Step 1 of the rollout rather than a detour around it.

04 Gate 3

Evidence, then repeat

Operators three through ten are not closed by a demo. They are closed by what happened to operator one.

WEEKS 12–16 Sell

Move to limited automation, then measure what changed

The AI begins sending clarifying questions and approved answers on its own. Everything else still drafts for a human. Meanwhile the pilot starts producing the only sales asset that really matters.

BUILD
  • Limited automation: clarifications and approved FAQs sent by the AI
  • First-response time, enquiries captured, handoffs — measured before and after
  • Tenant isolation hardened and tested with a second operator's data
  • Admin configuration, so operator two does not need you in the database
  • An onboarding path: connect a number, load knowledge, add staff
NOW WORTH ADDING
  • Follow-up automation, once opt-out handling is proven
  • A simple funnel view for the operator's manager
  • Quote drafting, if the pilot showed it was the bottleneck
WHAT THIS LETS YOU SELL

A number. “Their median first response went from four hours to ninety seconds, and they stopped losing weekend enquiries” closes operators in a way no demo does — and it is the first claim in this whole project that will be backed by measurement rather than estimate.

05 Scope

What to cut, and what never to cut

A solo build succeeds or fails on this page more than any other. Two things are worth protecting even while everything around them is being deferred.

Safe to defer
None of these are what an operator says yes to.
Build
  • Quote calculation and approval workflow
  • Follow-up automation
  • Lead scoring and prioritisation
  • Reporting dashboards
  • Arabic and mixed-language handling
  • Everything in the Operations MVP beyond the request queue
Do not cut, even for one customer
Both are cheap now and expensive to retrofit.
Calendar
  • An operator ID on every table, from the first migration. Retrofitting tenancy is the single most expensive item on this roadmap.
  • The eight safety rules, from day one. They cost almost nothing to build and they are the entire reason an operator trusts software near their customers.
Why these two specifically: everything else on the deferred list can be added later at roughly the same cost it would take today. These two get more expensive every week they are postponed — one because it touches every query you will have written by then, the other because the first unsupported promise to a real customer is the one that ends the pilot.
06 Calendar risks

The parts you cannot code faster

Three spans on this roadmap are not engineering time. Working longer hours does not shorten any of them, which is exactly why they need to start early.

START IN WEEK 1
Meta business verification

Business verification and template approval run on Meta's schedule, not yours. This is the likeliest way to end up with finished code and no number to run it on. Begin it before you write the first migration, and demo on a test number while it clears.

BEFORE GATE 2
Getting the operator's knowledge out of people's heads

Rates, deposits, driver requirements, delivery areas and the exceptions nobody wrote down. The rollout plan allots a week. It usually takes longer, because the answer currently lives with whoever has worked there longest — and the agent cannot answer anything you have not captured.

UNKNOWN UNTIL DONE
The prototype audit

The specification notes that the existing prototype's language, deployment and credentials have not been audited. If little turns out to be reusable, add a week or two to Gate 1. This is the one estimate here most likely to move, and it resolves in the first few days.

The failure mode is building all sixteen weeks before showing anyone.
Four months of solo work with no operator committed is the most common way a project like this ends. Gate 1 exists specifically to put something in front of a real buyer in month one, while the cost of being wrong is still four weeks rather than four months.
